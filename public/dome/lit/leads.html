Why it is here

The corpus's Bauhaus line stops in 1925–27 and stops in Germany. It holds Schlemmer's stage figures, the unrealisable projects he reclassified as materials for demonstration lectures, and Weininger's Kugeltheater — a sphere proposed as a theatre and never built. Schawinsky is what that line did when it crossed the Atlantic. A Bauhaus stage-workshop student, he taught Stage Studies at Black Mountain College from 1936, and with those students built the productions he later gathered under the word Spectodrama: a stage of space, shape, colour, light, movement, sound, language and music, in which some scenes carried pantomime and dance and others had no human performers at all, and which used projection and film among its means.

Two reasons it belongs in this review rather than in a footnote. First, it is the earliest projected-image stage practice the desk research has turned up in this lineage — its promoters place it roughly thirty years before 9 Evenings (1966), which is the date most writing on intermedia takes as the start. Second, its own advocates present it as an ancestor of the happening, and the person usually credited with the happening at Black Mountain is John Cage — who arrived there in 1948, more than a decade after Schawinsky left. That is a lineage claim, not a fact, and it is exactly the kind of claim a review should be able to test rather than repeat.

It also presses on the review's own genre. Spectodrama was developed inside a course, from a teaching practice Schawinsky called Stage Studies, in the same institution where the Bauhaus stage's own works were demonstrated and explained. Whether the demonstration-lecture form travelled with him — whether Stage Studies was taught as demonstration — is the specific question to put to his own account.

What the corpus already holds

Three traces, all thin, none of them a primary source:

  • "ALEXANDER SCHAWINSKY Scene from the Circus. First performance 1924 at the Bauhaus." — Gropius, The Theater of the Bauhaus, PDF p.67.
  • A plate credit, and the volume's only trace of him as a maker of stage images.
  • The essay text that follows that plate — films projected onto surfaces, the "ACTION OF LIGHT", the attack on the "peep-show stage" — is Moholy-Nagy's, in "Theater, Circus, Variety". The plate signature is what makes the attribution unstable, and the annotation says so.
  • "with additional images by Marcel Breuer, Kurt Schmidt, and Xanty Schawinsky" — Nikolić (2014), PDF p.17.
  • His presence among the makers of the 1925 volume's plates. Note the spelling in that article: Xanty.
  • "Schawinsky, Xanti. “From the Bauhaus to Black Mountain.” The Drama Review: TDR 15/3 (1971): 30–40." — Nikolić (2014), works cited, PDF p.20.
  • More useful than the plate credit: the primary text, printed in a bibliography already in the corpus.

Also in the volume, and not about his stage work: the 1961 acknowledgements (PDF p.107) thank "Xanti Schawinsky of New York City" among those who provided or located illustrations — evidence of his part in the book's construction, not of his place in its argument.

What a fulldome-performance review would need from it

  1. The apparatus question. Projection and film as stage means in 1936, in a room with an audience seated in front of it — the same apparatus the dome inherits, in the format the dome is defined against. This is the pre-history that the existing corpus (Grau, Manovich, Bay-Cheng) reaches for through panoramas and slide projectors, and Spectodrama is a live case rather than a lineage.
  2. The body question. Scenes with performers and scenes without, in one work: the body-as-instrument page has no precedent this early that also discusses its own means.
  3. The participation question. Moholy-Nagy's demand, in the same volume, that the stage stop producing "silent spectators" and let the audience "fuse with the action on the stage at the peak of catharsis" is already in this corpus. Whether Schawinsky's stage answered it is checkable.
  4. The genre question. Spectodrama's own documentation is a script, a set of photographs and a body of stage theory compiled decades later — the same situation as the dome's own works, whose masters go missing. Useful as an example of how a performance practice writes itself down.

Provenance

In the corpus, machine-verified: the Gropius plate credit (PDF p.67), the acknowledgements (PDF p.107) and the Nikolić lines (PDF pp.17, 20), all quotes checked against the extracted text of those PDFs.

From the web, not from anything read in the corpus: the Migros Museum Zürich exhibition page, the Mudam talk page, schawinsky.ch, and the Black Mountain College Museum + Arts Center pages. They agree on the outlines — Bauhaus stage workshop to Black Mountain in 1936, Stage Studies, projection and film among the means, pantomime in some scenes and no performers in others, the 1968–69 Play, Life, Illusion script — and disagree on nothing checked. The claims that it was "the world's first multimedia play" and that it anticipated the happening are theirs, not this corpus's, and are recorded here as assertions to test.

Not read: the TDR article and the retrospective volume. Nothing on this page is quoted from either. Until one of them is on disk, this stays a lead: the tool shows it dashed, and the coverage filter excludes it, because there is nothing here to read.
