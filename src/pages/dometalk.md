Hello! My name is Tara and I am a new media artist, animator, creative technologist, educator, organizer, researcher, writer and VJ depending on the day and on who's asking. Broadly, my work examines the way technology intervenes in our relationships with ourselves, each other, and our environments.

Today, for you, I am a fulldome artist. I'm here to talk to you about my film Flicker and Gambol.

It is my first fulldome film and it is an experimental dance animation about the cycles we move through and the full circles we encounter as we make our way forwards, the only direction we can go.

So you know what to expect — I'll talk to you about how I got here, share a bit of my process for the film, we'll watch the film, then I'll share what's next. Ok? Ok!

I'm experiencing a full circle moment right now.

One year ago, I saw Bertie Sampson and YeffYeff's Rapid Eye Movement during MUTEK, and realized everything I'd ever done suddenly made sense and maybe I had to dedicate my life to the dome? This is me taking notes in the club.

So I'm very happy to be here with you a year later, in the very dome that first blew my mind.

Ten years ago, I accidentally made a dome film but I never saw it. When I said Flicker and Gambol was my first, I lied, I'm so sorry. I took an animation class and got obsessed, often animating in square format because of a certain social media platform. A friend in the physics department did his senior thesis on fulldome and asked to include my work in the screening. This is what I made. But the new planetarium projection system was broken every time I went so I never got to see it in the dome, until now.

Things might have gone differently had the system worked, but instead my path arced outwards which is also a crucial part of a circle.

I continued to develop my practice as an animator by rotoscoping artists in my community in Oakland dancing, compositing them with fun backgrounds, and posting them on social media as a creative outlet and way to connect. After a particularly punishing rotoscoping client project, my wrist gave out, so I learned to work with AI to rotoscope using segmentation models and image to image models, which will come back later.

Meeting so many artists and becoming visible as an animator in my community led me to VJ for local acts and then a touring NFT music festival. Post pandemic was a wild time! And seeking to create more fully realized worlds, I creative directed a VR concept album which we later premiered at SXSW.

These experiences planted the seeds for my love of new media as a container for collective experience and connection.

And yet with each of these projects I felt frustrated with the limits of the medium. The worlds I was creating felt far away on stage or small seen through tiny digital windows or isolating in their context within a headset. I wanted to invite people in together but instead I was inviting them to take a peek or leave the world behind.

Returning to Canada two years ago, I started to find my voice in planar immersive animation at InterAccess in Toronto where I first started experimenting with some of the visuals in the film.

And then I entered the dome and I fell in love with it as a digital but collective medium. It remains tactile and embodied while enveloping us together in a shared experience of imagined worlds.

After MUTEK, I started exploring domemaster with every medium I'd been using. Marie, who I had met a year earlier in LA right before she would start her residency here, gave me my first dome advice — domeport allows you to simulate the dome, and also never use true black or you can see projection outlines — so I set about making things.

These were some things I made early on in Unreal, P5, and Midjourney, some of which I ended up using as backgrounds in the film. Then, adapting the same approach and visual language I'd been using for VJing and for MOVES, I made Flicker and Gambol.

This is an overview of the core of my process from motion capture to grooms and clothing to rendering clips to processing through image to image model to assembling with sound. But it begins with 3D animation.

For human movement I used motion capture, either of myself or pulled from mocap libraries applied to a metahuman. I added hair and a very rough clothing simulation so my characters would be dressed and have a more organic feel.

For the tiger and rabbit, I used 3D assets from existing libraries. And for the backgrounds I used some of my previously generated videos you saw before.

The masks were 3D models created from references to real life papier mâché masks that I'd made. For non human elements I did simple keyframe animation.

I rendered individual clips for each element (a person, animal, mask, tree), so I could play around with them collage style later, an approach I learned to love while VJing.

I took these clips as image sequences and then ran them through an image to image model, translating the hard surfaces into fauvism inspired frame by frame paintings.

I love playing with the inbetweens and cycles of digital and organic. It's what drew me to this approach in the first place because I could use AI as a sort of blurred collective consciousness to introduce an unpredictable organic element into otherwise quite prescriptive 3D animation.

I built scenes from all these clips in a circular format using 2D animation in After Effects, creating relationships between characters, entrances and exits, and overall building an abstract narrative from many parts.

Listening to Heal My Love by my friend and collaborator Graham Steinman, the pacing and narrative of the film clicked into place. We mixed for spatial audio at Oboro.

I've now had the privilege of having this film presented in Melbourne, Montevideo, Jena, and at Petit SAT this past spring. Thank you to the Ontario Arts Council for their support. I'm delighted to share it with you now, and then we'll circle back. Here is Flicker and Gambol.

Thanks for watching. I'm proud to share that last month it won the Janus Award for innovation at the Fulldome Festival Jena.

Now I'm thinking about other pieces of my practice that I'd love to full circle back around to.

I'm looking forward to returning to VJing for the first time since moving back to Canada at Venus Fest in Toronto this fall, here's a sneak peek.

I also have other projects in various stages of production that I would love to talk your ear off about and/or collaborate on including films, performances, interactive installations, as well as a domebook inspired by the pedagogical sketchbooks of Paul Klee.

You're going to see a lot more from me in the dome, but I also hope to see a lot more from you. Sharing knowledge feels very embedded in the dome ethos. Anything I can help you with or learn from you makes both of us better. And I'm curious, what circles are coming back around for you? Can't wait to talk to you all about dome :) thank you <3
